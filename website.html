The Last Days of the 80s Arcade

Posted on Nov 4, 2024 • 12 min read

Remember the smell of hot plastic and the crackle of the CRT? In the summer of 1987, I spent a week at the ElectroPlex Arcade in downtown San Francisco. The place was packed with kids who wore pixelated jackets and spoke in 8-bit English. I remember the Galaga cabinet had a blinking red indicator that said "READY" in block letters.

SYSTEM: ELECTROPLEX V1.0
CPU: Z80A @ 4.77MHz
RAM: 64KB
VIDEO: NTSC 256x240
STATUS: ONLINE
[SYSTEM CHECK: ALL SYSTEMS GO]

During that week, I discovered a hidden backdoor in the Dragon Slayer ROM. By modifying the jump table, I could trigger a secret level that wasn't listed in the instruction manual. The game would then display a message in ASCII art:

╔══════════════════════════════╗
║  WARNING: SYSTEM HACK DETECTED ║
║  YOU HAVE ACCESS TO ADMIN MODE  ║
║  PLEASE DISCONNECT IMMEDIATELY  ║
╚══════════════════════════════╝

Afterward, I never played that game again. The arcade owner said he'd "removed the anomaly" from the system. I suspect it was just a glitch in the ROM.

Today, we don't have arcade cabinets. We have VR headsets and cloud gaming. But the nostalgia remains. The simplicity of those days - a single controller, a single screen, a single sound - created a unique form of human connection.

Why I Still Use Floppy Disks

Posted on Nov 2, 2024 • 8 min read

Floppy disks are dying. The cloud has replaced them. But I still use them for one reason: they don't sync.

When you save something to a floppy disk, it's there. It's not waiting in some remote data center. It's not vulnerable to a network outage. If your router dies, your laptop dies, and the internet is down for three weeks, your data is still on that floppy disk in your drawer.

I keep a special collection of 5.25" and 3.5" disks in a locked drawer. Each one contains a different project: a homegrown text editor, a simple spreadsheet, a collection of poetry. I call this my "digital bunker."

DISK: POETRY_01_2024
FORMAT: MS-DOS F3.0
SIZE: 1.44MB
CREATION DATE: 2024-11-01
DESCRIPTION: Poems about rain, cities, and forgotten dreams.
[DISK CHECK: GOOD]

Some people think this is silly. But I've seen the internet fail. I've seen data centers go dark. The reliability of a spinning disk in a drawer is more reliable than the promise of cloud availability.

The Rise of the Keyboard Warrior

Posted on Oct 30, 2024 • 15 min read

There was a time when keyboard warriors didn't have a voice. They were just the silent background hum of the computer. Now, they speak.

Back in 1992, keyboard warriors were just the keys you pressed to navigate a DOS prompt. Today, they are the voices behind online arguments, the moderators of forums, the administrators of wikis. They are the ones who keep the digital world functioning.

But there's a danger. The more we rely on keyboard warriors, the more we lose the ability to reason through complex arguments. We don't think; we type. We don't listen; we respond.

┌────────────────────────────────────┐
│ USER: [REDACTED]                   │
│ ACTION: POST                       │
│ TARGET: /discussion/rainbow-hats  │
│ CONTENT: "Rainbow hats are stupid."│
└────────────────────────────────────┘

Perhaps the future of the keyboard warrior is not in argument, but in understanding. In learning to listen to the silence between the keys.
